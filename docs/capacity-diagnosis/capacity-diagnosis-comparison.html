Diagnosing a high-CU event: Capacity Metrics App vs FUAM

The same capacity, the same spike, worked through in both tools. Captured live on 2026-09-02 against tenant MngEnvMCAP777813, capacity Trial-Central64, with FUAM release 2026.6.1.

Peak utilization 45.06% Metrics App history 14 days FUAM history ~1 year Timepoint 30 s

The scenario

Utilization on Trial-Central64 rose to a sustained block between roughly 00:00 and 09:00 on 2026-09-01, peaking at 45.06% of base capacity. Nothing was throttled, so there is no error to chase — the question is only what consumed the CU.

Both tools can answer it. They differ in how many steps it takes, how far back you can ask, and what you can do with the answer afterwards.

Path A  Capacity Metrics App

1
Health page — find the capacity. Lists every capacity you administer with average utilization and throttling counts. Here Trial-Central64 is Healthy at 6.14% average, no throttling. This page tells you nothing is on fire; it does not tell you what is running.
2
Compute page — find the spike. Fourteen days of history. The ribbon chart on the left ranks items by CU; the utilization chart on the right shows the spike near Sep 01. Peak utilization reads 45.06%. The items table underneath is a 14-day total, not the spike.
3
Filter to a single day. Selecting a date on the ribbon chart switches the utilization chart from a six-minute peak rollup to every 30-second timepoint. Without this step you cannot select an individual timepoint.
4
Drill through to Timepoint Detail. Select a timepoint column, then use Explore – TimePoint Detail. This is the answer: one 30-second window, split into interactive and background tables, with per-operation CU.

The answer. The top consumer in that window is pocportal_events, an Eventstream in FabricPOCPortal — 28,462.6 total CU(s), contributing 9.88 CU(s) to this timepoint, 8.24% of base capacity on its own. Every operation is background; the interactive table is empty. The burndown table is all zeroes, so nothing is carrying forward.
Cost of getting here. Five interactions across three report pages, and two of them are not discoverable: you must know to filter the ribbon chart before timepoint selection is possible, and the drill-through button stays greyed out until a column is selected. The window is fixed at 14 days.

Path B  FUAM

1
Home — pick an analytical pathway. FUAM opens on a map of what it has collected, with named pathways underneath. One of them is Item Outliers based on CU usage, which is the question being asked here.
2
Capacity Utilization — every capacity, a year at a time. Small multiples, one per capacity, on a date axis running 2025-09-22 to 2026-09-01. The guided header states step 1 and step 2 explicitly. Note the spikes above 5,000% on fabriccentracapacity — events the Metrics App can no longer show at all.
3
The daily tooltip. Hovering a day returns max and average timepoint CU%, throttling counts, the SKU in force, the utilization curve, all three throttling charts and overages — on one card. Max TimePoint CU% reads 45.06%, matching the Metrics App exactly.
4
Item Outlier Analysis. Ranks items by two measures the Metrics App does not compute: Daily CU vs 14d Avg CU, which flags an item consuming more than its own baseline, and an Outlier Factor derived from standard variance, so a large item is not flagged merely for being large.
5
Item Operations, daily. Operation counts and CU by item kind over the whole retained period, including failure, cancelled, invalid and rejected counts and max throttling minutes.

Why that table was empty — and what it reveals. The item-level data for 2026-07-04 is not missing: FUAM holds 17 item/operation rows and 250,761 CU(s) for that day, topped by an LlmPlugin AI Query at 13,177 CU(s) over 18 operations. The table renders empty because 100% of that day's CU belongs to workspaces that have since been deleted, and the report carries a report-level filter Is Capacity Deleted on fuam_deleted. In this tenant 146 of 295 workspaces are flagged deleted — mostly short-lived autorunner_* automation workspaces. By contrast, 0% of 2026-09-01's CU maps to deleted workspaces, which is why the recent day renders normally.
The practical consequence. If your workload creates and destroys workspaces — CI runs, per-customer provisioning, ephemeral test environments — FUAM's long history is retained in the Lakehouse but hidden in its reports. Recovering it means querying the tables directly, which is exactly what the Lakehouse SQL endpoint is for. The finding above came from a DAX query against capacity_metrics_by_item_by_operation_by_day, not from any report page.

Side by side

Capacity Metrics App

  • Microsoft-supported, installed from AppSource
  • ~10–15 minute latency, closest to real time
  • Per-operation detail down to a 30-second timepoint
  • Fixed 14-day compute window, 30-day storage
  • No alerting, no cross-capacity history
  • Semantic model is not supported for querying

FUAM

  • Community accelerator, not Microsoft-supported
  • As fresh as the last pipeline run — daily here
  • Item and operation detail for every extracted day
  • Reports hide deleted workspaces; the Lakehouse keeps them
  • Outlier Factor and CU-vs-baseline, computed
  • Lands in a Lakehouse: queryable by SQL and DAX

They are complements, not alternatives. The Metrics App answers what is happening now, in detail. FUAM answers what keeps happening, and to whom. Neither alerts; that needs Fabric capacity overview events streamed to an Eventhouse.
FUAM is not free. Measured on this deployment, its own pipeline consumed 61,122 CU(s) across five active days, peaking at 23,116 CU(s) — 13.4% of an F2's daily budget, 0.42% of an F64's. On a small capacity, budget for it before installing.
